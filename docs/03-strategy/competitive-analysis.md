# Competitive analysis

Research snapshot: **August 21, 2026**. Product pages and store listings can change; recheck before investor, launch, or positioning claims.

## 1. Summary

### Observed

The market already contains:

- private cooling-off timers and savings histories;
- guided Buy/Wait/Skip checklists;
- public or category-specific purchase voting;
- AI purchase advisers;
- post-purchase follow-up and pattern learning.

### Inference

Before I Buy cannot credibly differentiate through any one of those features. Its strongest unvalidated territory is the full social outcome loop: friends predict, the owner later reports lived satisfaction, everyone receives a Reveal, predictions resolve, and the owner accumulates transparent personal insight.

### Recommendation

Lead with **“what did Future You actually think?”**, not “stop impulse buying,” “ask the internet,” or “AI decides.” Prove the delayed Reveal before building a public community.

## 3. Competitor profiles

### Wait Before You Buy

**Observed:** customizable waiting periods, progress, Buy/Skip decision, saved-money total, skip streak, categories, product links, and local/no-account storage.

**Strengths:** simple mental model, privacy, low infrastructure cost, clear impulse-control value.

**Weaknesses/inference:** utility is episodic and individual; “money saved” implicitly makes Skip the preferred outcome; no social reason to return between personal temptations.

**Before I Buy must differ by:** making a good purchase equally valid and closing a social outcome loop.

### CutCut

**Observed:** a “Circuit Breaker” asks questions about use, duplication, need, and future desire; the product offers waitlists, analytics, reminder personalities, and premium tiers.

**Strengths:** polished reflection framing, configurable tone, clear premium analytics path.

**Weaknesses/inference:** no friend prediction layer; assertive anti-impulse positioning may not appeal to users who want permission for good spending.

**Before I Buy must differ by:** friend context, neutral satisfaction framing, and outcome-based social learning.

### Should I Buy This?

**Observed:** a short checklist returns Buy, Wait 48 Hours, Save For It, or Skip; it keeps history and insights while claiming on-device privacy and no accounts/servers.

**Strengths:** extremely clear, private, fast, and easy to understand.

**Weaknesses/inference:** system verdict replaces social judgment; insights focus on smart-choice/saved-money framing.

**Before I Buy must differ by:** not issuing an authoritative verdict and making later friend participation valuable.

### Should U Buy

**Observed:** users post item, price, photo/link, and a pitch; the internet votes Buy, Skip, or Wait for Sale. The site emphasizes no accounts, sharing, virality, and fast public verdicts.

**Strengths:** low-friction social mechanic, public content supply, humorous identity.

**Weaknesses/inference:** anonymous judgment may lack personal context; current copy can become harsh or shaming; the verdict appears to be the endpoint rather than the start of a longitudinal loop.

**Before I Buy must differ by:** trusted audience, later lived outcome, prediction resolution, and non-judgmental language.

### Buy or Bye

**Observed:** fashion users post outfits, hauls, or potential purchases and receive community voting, ratings, comments, and “roasts.”

**Strengths:** visually rich, frequent content category, community identity.

**Weaknesses/inference:** narrow category and deliberately blunt feedback increase appearance/social-comparison risks; no observed satisfaction follow-up.

**Before I Buy must differ by:** cross-category usefulness, owner-controlled privacy, and future satisfaction rather than taste popularity.

### FourTip

**Observed:** four AI personalities analyze images/screenshots, discuss value and motivation, suggest alternatives, follow up after purchase, and claim to help users learn decision patterns.

**Strengths:** immediate response, low social cold start, product-input automation, longitudinal ambition.

**Weaknesses/inference:** AI advice can feel generic or falsely personal before sufficient history; it removes the real-friend game loop.

**Before I Buy must differ by:** human context and transparent evidence. AI should eventually reduce friction or synthesize, not become the authority.

## 4. Category lessons

| Category | Table stakes | Opportunity | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cooling-off | Configurable timer, reminder, decision | Tie pause to original reason and social anticipation | Too much friction kills posting |
| Private decision tool | Fast inputs, history, privacy | Satisfaction rather than savings | Episodic use |
| Social voting | Link sharing, instant vote, visible results | Trusted friends and later outcome | Judgment, spam, empty feed |
| AI adviser | Image/link extraction, quick response | Invisible assistance after validation | Authority, hallucination, privacy |
| Post-purchase tracking | Follow-up and patterns | Reveal to earlier participants | Completion decay over time |
| Gamification | Scores, streaks, identity | Resolved prediction reputation | Performative or harsh behavior |

## 5. Positioning alternatives

### Recommended

**A social decision game about what Future You will actually value.**

Why: names the social and longitudinal difference without promising financial optimization.

### Secondary

**Ask friends now. Find out later who understood Future You.**

Why: emphasizes the game, but may over-focus on prediction competition.

### Reject

- “Stop impulse buying.” Crowded and makes Skip the goal.
- “The internet decides.” Crowded, unsafe, and removes agency.
- “AI shopping adviser.” Directly overlaps FourTip and similar tools.
- “Social budgeting.” Implies oversharing and a different product category.

## 6. Defensibility thesis

Features alone are copyable. Potential defensibility, if earned, comes from:

1. a dense graph of trusted purchase-decision relationships;
2. resolved longitudinal data connecting initial reason, peer prediction, decision, and satisfaction;
3. a tone and privacy model that makes users willing to share authentic dilemmas;
4. a habit of returning for outcomes, not endless content consumption.

These assets only emerge after repeated completed loops. The MVP should optimize completion quality, not top-of-funnel volume.

## 7. Research gaps

- No reliable market-size estimate was established.
- Store listings do not prove retention, engagement, or commercial traction.
- The research did not validate willingness to expose item price to friends.
- It did not establish that delayed Reveal is novel in every market.
- It did not compare Brazilian Portuguese alternatives comprehensively.

Recheck these gaps before fundraising or making public uniqueness claims.
