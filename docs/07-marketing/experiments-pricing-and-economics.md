# Experiments, pricing, and economics

## 1. Objective

Find a revenue model that increases sustainable value without weakening authentic dilemmas, free participation, reflection completion, or trust.

All prices below are hypotheses for research, not final commercial decisions.

## 2. Preconditions for charging

Do not launch paid features until:

- the core closed-loop beta passes its product gates;
- users demonstrate repeat creation or repeat participation;
- enough users accumulate history for a premium insight preview;
- cancellation, refund, entitlement, restore, and deletion flows are tested;
- privacy and consumer-law review is complete;
- the free loop remains complete;
- monetization guardrail metrics have baselines.

## 3. Price hypotheses for Brazil

Test willingness to pay before selecting a price. Suggested research cells:

| Offer | Monthly hypothesis | Annual hypothesis | What the test learns |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Future You Plus | R$ 9.90 / 14.90 / 19.90 | Approximately 8–10 monthly payments | Individual value sensitivity |
| Circle Pass | R$ 19.90 / 29.90 / 39.90 | Approximately 8–10 monthly payments | Willingness to sponsor group value |
| Creator Pro | R$ 39 / 69 / 99 plus optional take rate | Annual discount later | Host-tool value before marketplace liquidity |
| Cosmetic pack | R$ 4.90 / 9.90 / 14.90 once | Not applicable | Expression and gifting demand |
| Premium recap | R$ 9.90 / 19.90 once | Included in Plus | One-time willingness to pay without subscription |

Do not infer willingness to pay from a “Would you pay?” survey alone. Use concept ranking, price-sensitivity questions, fake-door tests, and ultimately a small real-payment pilot with clear refund terms.

## 4. Subscription packaging experiments

### Experiment M1 — Premium value ranking

**Question:** Which benefits feel valuable enough to pay for?

Show separate concepts for:

- advanced personal insight;
- annual recap and export;
- profile/circle cosmetics;
- multiple circles and group recap;
- private calibration and Illusion Filter trends.

**Success signal:** at least two benefits consistently rank as must-have among users with five or more resolved dilemmas.

**Failure signal:** users only value features needed to complete the free loop.

### Experiment M2 — Individual versus Circle Pass

**Question:** Does the group create more willingness to pay than the individual archive?

Randomize established groups to see:

- Future You Plus;
- Circle Pass sponsored by one member;
- a combined offer.

Measure stated preference, checkout start, completed purchase, and subsequent group participation.

**Guardrail:** non-paying members must not feel demoted or excluded.

### Experiment M3 — Monthly versus annual framing

**Question:** Is the product naturally understood as occasional annual memory value or ongoing monthly utility?

Compare:

- monthly-first;
- annual-first with transparent equivalent monthly price;
- one-time premium recap.

**Guardrail:** no fake discount deadline or preselected annual plan.

### Experiment M4 — Cosmetic expression

**Question:** Will adults pay for tasteful identity and Reveal presentation?

Test three visual directions:

- understated editorial;
- playful social;
- artist collaboration.

**Success signal:** purchase intent and actual conversion without negative “childish” or “pay-to-win” feedback.

### Experiment M5 — Creator Circle pilot

**Question:** Can a creator deliver recurring decision-community value independent of product promotion?

Pilot with a small set of adult creators in categories such as personal style, home organization, hobbies, technology literacy, or travel planning.

Measure:

- free-to-paid conversion;
- paid-member retention;
- dilemma and reflection completion;
- moderation workload;
- commercial-disclosure comprehension;
- whether members feel pressured to consume.

### Experiment M6 — Appreciation gift distortion

**Question:** Does paid appreciation change prediction quality or tone?

Run only after a free thank-you mechanic is established. Compare communities with and without optional paid gifts.

Stop if:

- extreme or performative votes increase;
- users solicit gifts;
- high spend becomes socially visible;
- helpfulness becomes correlated with telling owners what they want to hear.

## 5. Monetization funnel

### Subscription

`eligible history → premium preview viewed → offer viewed → checkout started → trial/purchase → first premium value used → renewal → cancellation/reactivation`

### Circle Pass

`active circle → shared premium preview → sponsor offer → purchase → members activate benefit → circle repeats → renewal`

### Creator Circle

`creator profile viewed → free room sampled → membership offer → purchase → first member benefit → recurring participation → renewal`

### Cosmetics

`customization opened → pack previewed → purchase → applied → retained/shared/gifted`

## 6. Core revenue metrics

### Demand

- eligible-user offer-view rate;
- checkout-start rate;
- paid conversion by accumulated resolved dilemmas;
- trial-to-paid conversion;
- premium feature activation;
- Circle Pass sponsor conversion;
- cosmetic purchase and gift rate;
- creator free-to-paid conversion.

### Retention

- month-one and month-three subscriber retention;
- annual renewal intent and realized renewal;
- premium feature reuse;
- paid circle activity and completion;
- creator paid-member retention;
- voluntary cancellation reason.

### Economics

- gross revenue;
- net revenue after store/payment fees, taxes, refunds, and creator share;
- average revenue per paying user;
- average revenue per active group;
- contribution margin by product;
- support, moderation, and media/AI cost per payer;
- customer acquisition cost only after organic retention is proven;
- payback period and lifetime-value range, reported with assumptions.

## 7. Trust and product guardrails

| Guardrail | Why it matters | Stop/review trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Free-loop completion | Paid design must not damage the core product | Material decline versus pre-monetization baseline |
| Reflection completion | Revenue must not shift attention away from outcomes | Sustained relative decline |
| Honest-dilemma rate | Users must still feel safe posting real uncertainty | Interview or behavioral evidence of performative posting |
| Non-payer participation | Social value depends on free friends | Vote or Reveal decline among non-payers |
| Paywall comprehension | Users must understand free versus paid | Repeated confusion or complaints |
| Cancellation success | Subscription must be easy to leave | Any systematic failure or dark-pattern complaint |
| Purchase-regret prompt safety | Do not sell at vulnerable moments | Any monetization impression in prohibited contexts |
| Commercial disclosure comprehension | Sponsored content must be obvious | Less than 90% recognition in usability testing |
| Privacy trust | Private decisions are the product foundation | Any belief that normal dilemmas are sold to brands |
| Status anxiety | Paid cosmetics must not create hierarchy | Qualitative harm or reduced participation |

Exact thresholds should be set from beta baselines before launching each experiment.

## 8. Unit-economics template

Use a scenario model rather than one optimistic forecast.

### Per paying user

`Net monthly revenue = listed price − app-store/payment fees − taxes − refunds − creator share`

`Contribution margin = net monthly revenue − variable infrastructure − customer support − moderation − variable AI cost`

`Payback months = acquisition cost ÷ monthly contribution margin`

`Simple LTV = monthly contribution margin ÷ monthly payer churn`

The simple LTV formula is highly sensitive to churn and should not be presented as precise when cohorts are small.

### Scenarios

Model at least:

- conservative: low conversion, high churn, full store fee, high support;
- base: observed pilot conversion and cohort churn;
- upside: stronger Circle Pass and annual mix, not artificially lower safety costs.

Never remove moderation, refunds, taxes, or store fees to make the upside scenario attractive.

## 9. Revenue-quality scorecard

Evaluate each revenue stream on more than revenue:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| User value | Would the paid feature still feel valuable if it did not increase engagement? |
| Incentive alignment | Does revenue grow when users learn, or only when they buy more? |
| Trust | Can users explain who pays, who benefits, and what data is used? |
| Social health | Does the model preserve participation by non-payers? |
| Revenue durability | Is value recurring or dependent on novelty? |
| Operational burden | What moderation, support, fraud, and creator-management work follows? |
| Platform exposure | How sensitive is margin to store rules and fees? |
| Reversibility | Can the experiment be removed without damaging user history or access? |

A model should not progress if incentive alignment or trust is rated low, even if early conversion is high.

## 10. Commercial implementation checklist

- [ ] Free entitlements and paid entitlements documented
- [ ] Prices localized and taxes/store treatment reviewed
- [ ] Trial, renewal, cancellation, refund, restore, and grace-period flows tested
- [ ] Subscription offers state price, cadence, benefits, and renewal clearly
- [ ] No prechecked marketing or data-sharing consent
- [ ] Family/circle payer cannot access members' private information
- [ ] Purchased cosmetics remain available according to disclosed terms
- [ ] Revenue events exclude private dilemma content
- [ ] Creator payout, fraud, chargeback, tax, and moderation policies documented
- [ ] Sponsored content labeled before interaction
- [ ] Affiliate links, if any, labeled beside the action
- [ ] Safety, deletion, report, and block controls remain free
- [ ] Accessibility applies to checkout, entitlements, and paid assets
- [ ] App-store review requirements and current Brazilian legal obligations reviewed before launch

## 11. Decision recommendation

Start with one paid hypothesis:

> **Future You Plus:** advanced private insight, recap, archive, export, and expression for users who have already completed enough decisions to see the value.

Then test whether the social unit is more powerful than the individual unit:

> **Circle Pass:** one person pays to make a trusted group's shared memory and identity richer, without controlling anyone else's data.

Defer creator commerce, gifts, affiliate links, advertising, and brand programs until subscription and group-value experiments reveal whether the product can sustain itself without conflicted purchase incentives.
