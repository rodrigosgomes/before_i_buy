# Monetization strategy

## 1. Strategic thesis

Before I Buy should monetize the value created **around** a purchase decision, not the transaction itself.

The strongest paid value is:

- a richer memory of what the user wanted and what happened;
- clearer personal patterns across completed decisions;
- social identity and expression inside trusted groups;
- better tools for people who host active circles or creator communities;
- celebratory artifacts users want to keep or share;
- convenience that does not weaken the free closed loop.

The weakest fit is revenue that increases when users buy more, share more personal data, or feel more anxious. That would put the business model in conflict with the product promise.

## 2. Lessons from social and creator platforms

Current social platforms commonly monetize five kinds of value:

1. **Exclusive access and recurring community value.** Instagram Subscriptions let creators charge monthly for exclusive content and benefits; Discord Server Subscriptions package community access into tiers; Patreon combines membership tiers with structured benefits. ([Instagram](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/406451644272016), [Discord](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/4415163187607-Server-Subscriptions-for-Members), [Patreon](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/203913559-How-to-set-up-paid-tiers-and-benefits))
2. **Visible identity and belonging.** YouTube memberships use public badges, custom emoji, exclusive posts, and early access to make membership socially legible. ([YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14917424?hl=en))
3. **Appreciation and recognition.** Reddit connects awards and community recognition to qualifying contributor earnings; Instagram lets viewers send Gifts on Reels. ([Reddit](https://redditinc.com/policies/contributor-monetization-policy-2-2), [Instagram Gifts](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/738469380549477/?locale=en_GB))
4. **Creator–brand collaboration.** TikTok supports brand partnerships and requires commercial-content disclosure, including labels such as Paid partnership. ([TikTok](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/promoting-a-brand-product-or-service?invalid_lang=ms))
5. **Digital goods and subscriptions.** App stores support subscriptions, non-consumables, consumables, and gifting, but require clear value, pricing, and purchase disclosures. ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

**Inference for Before I Buy:** the transferable pattern is not an infinite advertising feed. It is a layered economy of membership, identity, recognition, group utility, and creator tooling.

## 3. Monetization principles

1. **Free loop completeness.** Creation, voting, decision, reflection, and Reveal remain usable without payment.
2. **Charge for accumulated value.** Premium becomes more valuable as a user builds history; it must not withhold a current outcome.
3. **Cosmetics do not alter truth.** Paid themes, frames, and reactions cannot change prediction scores, reach, or analytics.
4. **Influence is earned, not bought.** A user may pay for tools and presentation, never for a higher accuracy rank or undisclosed distribution.
5. **Commercial content is unmistakable.** Brand-funded content is labeled before interaction and separated from organic dilemmas.
6. **No private-data resale.** Private dilemmas, prices, reasons, votes, and reflection histories are not sold to advertisers or data brokers.
7. **Easy cancellation and honest pricing.** No preselected add-ons, hidden fees, confusing trials, or cancellation friction. The FTC identifies disguised ads, buried terms, difficult cancellation, and tricked data sharing as dark-pattern risks. ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light))
8. **Emotion creates meaning, not coercion.** Vanity, influence, curiosity, and belonging may support expression and connection; they must not be converted into shame, scarcity pressure, or compulsive use.

## 4. Revenue portfolio

### 4.1 Future You Plus — recommended first model

**Model:** monthly or annual consumer subscription.

**Value proposition:** “Keep a clearer memory of what Future You actually values.”

Potential paid benefits:

- advanced history filters and search;
- category, reason, and price-band pattern cards after minimum samples;
- private Illusion Filter trends across owner expectation, friend predictions, and outcome;
- annual and seasonal decision recaps;
- export to PDF/CSV and durable personal archive;
- custom private circles and circle themes;
- additional Reveal themes, profile frames, and reaction packs;
- cross-device draft continuity and enhanced media organization;
- later, carefully reviewed AI summaries grounded only in the user's own resolved history.

Keep free:

- at least one active circle;
- the full core loop;
- basic chronological history;
- essential privacy, accessibility, deletion, and safety controls;
- every Reveal for participants;
- corrections to decisions and reflections.

**Why it fits:** recurring value grows from memory and self-knowledge, not from causing more purchases.

**Primary emotional value:** satisfaction, mastery, self-expression, and reflective vanity.

**Main risk:** the product may not generate enough monthly value at individual purchase frequency. Counter this with annual packaging, group value, and high-quality recaps—not streak pressure.

### 4.2 Circle Pass — recommended second model

**Model:** one subscriber sponsors premium benefits for a private group, similar in structure to a community membership.

Potential benefits:

- custom circle name, visual identity, and reactions;
- shared monthly or seasonal recap;
- private category specialties such as “tech oracle” or “style instinct,” awarded from minimum resolved samples;
- group challenges that reward completion and reflection, not buying or skipping;
- longer shared history and searchable past decisions;
- additional member capacity if validated as meaningful;
- rotating host or curator tools.

Everyone in the circle receives the social benefit; only the sponsor pays. This reduces the awkwardness of individual paywalls inside a friendship group.

**Primary emotional value:** belonging, group pride, reciprocity, and influence.

**Main risk:** a payer could feel ownership over friends' data. The Circle Pass grants presentation and organization features, never access to private fields or control over member histories.

### 4.3 Cosmetic expression and celebration — recommended experiment

**Model:** one-time purchases or subscription-included digital goods.

Examples:

- profile frames and circle emblems;
- themed Reveal animations with a reduced-motion alternative;
- sticker and reaction packs;
- shareable recap layouts;
- collectible, non-random “eras” or category themes;
- giftable cosmetic packs for a friend or circle;
- milestone cards for completed reflections or well-calibrated predictions.

Use transparent, direct purchase. Do not use randomized loot boxes, expiring purchased currency, or fake scarcity. Apple requires odds disclosure for paid randomized items; the stronger product choice here is to avoid randomness entirely. ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

**Primary emotional value:** vanity, identity, celebration, and gifting.

**Main risk:** cosmetics could make the app feel childish. Test language and visual style with the adult Brazilian target audience.

### 4.4 Creator Circles — later, after creator/community validation

**Model:** creators charge members for a recurring decision community; Before I Buy keeps a platform fee.

Possible creator offerings:

- themed decision rooms such as capsule wardrobe, desk setup, travel choices, or hobby gear;
- scheduled “purchase court” sessions;
- members-only creator dilemmas and post-decision breakdowns;
- educational challenges such as “wait 72 hours and compare”; 
- creator-curated reflection prompts and decision templates;
- community prediction seasons with private or opt-in public recognition;
- analytics on participation and completion, excluding private user data.

Platform revenue:

- percentage of creator subscription revenue;
- optional creator-pro tool subscription;
- payment processing pass-through disclosed separately.

Creator earnings should reward community value and completion, not conversion to purchases. Any sponsored creator activity must be labeled clearly. Brazilian government guidance referencing CONAR emphasizes clear, visible identification of advertising and genuine representations of experience. ([Brazilian government guidance](https://www.gov.br/secom/pt-br/assuntos/publicidade/publicidade-no-governo-federal/meios/internet/produtores-de-conteudo))

**Primary emotional value:** influence, expertise, belonging, and access.

**Main risks:** hidden advertising, parasocial pressure, moderation burden, and conflicts between trusted advice and commerce.

### 4.5 Appreciation gifts — conditional experiment

**Model:** participants send a small paid appreciation item after a Reveal; qualifying creators or community hosts may receive a share.

Safer design:

- gifts happen only after the decision and reflection;
- the sender sees the exact price before confirmation;
- gifts celebrate helpfulness, kindness, or entertainment—not “making” someone buy or skip;
- no gift leaderboard;
- no public display of gift value;
- no prompts targeted at likely high spenders;
- strict age, refund, fraud, and payout controls.

**Primary emotional value:** gratitude and reciprocity.

**Main risk:** advice becomes performative or financially motivated. Stop if paid reactions distort voting behavior.

### 4.6 Brand-funded decision challenges — high-risk, later only

**Model:** a brand funds a clearly labeled challenge, research panel, or educational experience.

Potential formats:

- “Use what you own for seven days before deciding” challenge;
- repair-versus-replace reflection;
- product-category education that does not recommend a brand outcome;
- opt-in concept testing with adult participants and a fixed reward unrelated to answers;
- sponsored cosmetic theme whose purchase or use has no effect on visibility.

Required separation:

- a distinct commercial surface, never disguised as an organic friend dilemma;
- label before participation, not only afterward;
- no brand access to identifiable private dilemmas;
- no reward conditional on Buy, positive sentiment, or sharing contacts;
- independent moderation and measurement;
- brand cannot alter negative results or Reveal language.

**Primary emotional value:** participation, novelty, identity, and contribution.

**Main risk:** the app loses credibility as a neutral reflection tool. Treat trust metrics as hard stop conditions.

### 4.7 Post-decision affiliate links — possible but not recommended early

**Model:** after an owner independently records a decision, the app may offer an optional merchant link and receive commission.

If ever tested:

- never show affiliate commerce before the owner records the decision;
- never change vote ranking, reflection timing, or insight language;
- label the commercial relationship next to the link;
- offer a non-affiliate path where practical;
- do not claim the app verified savings or product quality;
- report affiliate revenue separately from product-health metrics;
- allow users to disable commercial links.

This remains strategically conflicted because revenue rises with transactions. It should be inferior in priority to subscriptions, Circle Pass, cosmetics, and creator tools.

### 4.8 Aggregated research products — separate opt-in product or reject

Brands may value aggregated reasons, desire decay, and regret patterns. Using normal private dilemmas for this purpose is not acceptable.

A potentially acceptable version would be a separate, explicit adult research panel with:

- separate enrollment and compensation terms;
- purpose-specific questions;
- no reuse of private friend-group data;
- minimum cohort sizes and disclosure controls;
- participant withdrawal and deletion;
- independent privacy and legal review.

If the company cannot maintain that separation, reject the model.

## 5. Prioritization matrix

| Model | Strategic fit | Revenue potential | Trust risk | Complexity | Recommended stage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Future You Plus | High | Medium-high | Low-medium | Medium | First paid pilot |
| Circle Pass | High | Medium | Low-medium | Medium | Second paid pilot |
| Cosmetics and recaps | Medium-high | Medium | Low | Medium | Alongside/after subscription |
| Creator Circles | Medium-high | High | High | High | After community validation |
| Appreciation gifts | Medium | Medium | High | High | Controlled experiment |
| Brand-funded challenges | Medium | High | Very high | High | Late and separately governed |
| Post-decision affiliate links | Low-medium | Medium-high | Very high | Medium | Defer |
| Conventional feed advertising | Low | High at scale | Very high | High | Reject for private product |
| Sale of private behavioral data | Negative | Potentially high | Critical | High | Reject |
| Pay-to-win prediction boosts | Negative | Medium | Critical | Medium | Reject |

## 6. Packaging hypothesis

### Free

- complete decision loop;
- basic history and prediction record;
- one core private circle;
- standard profile and Reveal style;
- essential reminders and privacy controls.

### Future You Plus

- richer private insights and long-range history;
- premium recap and export;
- multiple or enhanced circles;
- cosmetic library;
- private calibration and Illusion Filter trends;
- later, grounded AI assistance if separately validated.

### Circle Pass

- shared premium identity and recap;
- group challenge tools;
- group history and category specialties;
- benefits shared with invited members without exposing private data.

### Creator Pro

- paid community configuration;
- templates, scheduling, moderation, and engagement analytics;
- subscription and payout tools;
- commercial disclosure workflow;
- no purchase-conversion optimization.

## 7. Recommended sequence

### Stage A — prove willingness to pay without charging

- concept-test premium recap, filters, export, themes, and Circle Pass;
- use fake-door entry only if clearly labeled as research and no payment details are collected;
- interview users who complete at least five resolved dilemmas;
- test annual and monthly framing.

### Stage B — small paid subscription pilot

- one simple Future You Plus tier;
- transparent monthly and annual options;
- seven- or fourteen-day trial only with explicit renewal terms and easy cancellation;
- no discounted urgency countdown;
- grandfather early testers where reasonable.

### Stage C — group and cosmetic expansion

- test Circle Pass with established groups;
- introduce direct-purchase themes and gifting;
- verify that payment does not reduce free participation or create status anxiety.

### Stage D — creator economy

- invite a small number of adult creators in low-risk categories;
- require sponsorship disclosure and moderation readiness;
- start with subscriptions and tools, not affiliate conversion.

### Stage E — commercial programs

- consider brand challenges or post-decision affiliate links only after independent trust review;
- maintain a visible commercial-content registry and complaint process;
- stop if users become less willing to post honest dilemmas.

## 8. Strategic recommendation

The best business is a **subscription-supported social memory product**, strengthened by group membership, identity cosmetics, and later creator tools.

The app should make money when users say:

> “This helps me understand myself and makes my group more fun.”

It should not make money because users bought more products, exposed more private information, or felt pressured to maintain status.
